# Talk Overview


Despite a long history of Arab-Chinese interactions dating back to the seventh century, modern Arab knowledge production on China has developed relatively recently.


One major reason for this limited scholarship is the historical dominance of Western-centered knowledge systems. As a result, many Arab scholars have traditionally examined their own societies through relationships with the West, while fewer have focused on China as another major non-Western reference point.


Another important challenge is linguistic. Conducting research on China requires Arabic-speaking scholars to acquire Chinese, a particularly demanding language-learning process. Although some scholars have achieved this expertise, much of their research has remained published primarily in Arabic and has therefore received limited international attention.


---

# Research Focus


This colloquium examines and contextualizes modern Arabic-language scholarship on China by introducing major institutions, scholars, and representative works to a broader international audience.


Professor Wen will discuss:

- The historical formation of Arab scholarship on China
- The development of China studies in Arabic-language academic communities
- Current challenges facing the field
- The significance of Arab perspectives on China in the context of growing China-Arab engagement in the twenty-first century


---

# About the Researcher


Professor Wen's forthcoming first monograph investigates the transformative processes of Arab-Chinese entanglements during the age of global empires, from the mid-nineteenth century to the end of World War II.


Before entering academia, Professor Wen worked as a broadcast journalist for Phoenix Satellite Television InfoNews Channel in Hong Kong (2003–2006), covering major international events in the Middle East.


She also worked as an English-Mandarin-Cantonese simultaneous interpreter for live news coverage.
