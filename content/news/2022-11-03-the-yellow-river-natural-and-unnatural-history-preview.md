# Talk Overview


In this talk, Professor Ruth Mostern examines the Yellow River as both a natural system and a product of human activity.


The river has long been central to China's historical development, while also presenting challenges through floods, environmental changes, and large-scale human interventions.


By exploring the interaction between ecological forces and human societies, the talk provides a new perspective on how landscapes are shaped by both nature and history.


---

# About the Speaker's Research


Professor Mostern is the author of:


*Dividing the Realm in Order to Govern: The Spatial Organization of the Song State, 960–1276 CE*  
(Harvard Asia Center, 2011)


and


*The Yellow River: A Natural and Unnatural History*  
(Yale University Press, 2021)


She is also co-editor of:


*Placing Names: Enriching and Integrating Gazetteers*  
(Indiana University Press, 2016)


and a special issue of *Open Rivers Journal* (2017).


Her research has resulted in more than thirty articles published in books and peer-reviewed journals.


---

# Digital Humanities and Historical Research


Professor Mostern is the Principal Investigator and Project Director of the **World Historical Gazetteer**, a digital infrastructure platform designed to integrate databases of historical place-name information.


Through combining historical research and digital methods, the project supports new ways of understanding connections between places, societies, and historical change.
