# Bars as Neighborhood Third Places in Tokyo and Shanghai


![Event Poster](/images/events/bars-third-places-2026/poster.png)


The Duke Kunshan University Colloquium Committee (UCC) presents:

**"Bars as Neighborhood Third Places in Tokyo and Shanghai."**


For decades, drinking bars and small dining bars have played a central but often overlooked role in neighborhood social life across Tokyo and Shanghai.


This talk examines how neighborhood bars function as "third places"—accessible social spaces outside homes and workplaces where urban residents build connections, maintain community ties, and experience everyday forms of belonging.

# Talk Overview


Unlike high-end entertainment districts that often dominate urban planning narratives, neighborhood bars—including Japanese izakaya in postwar yokochō alleyways and small bars on Shanghai residential streets—have historically served as accessible spaces of sociability across class and gender lines.


Drawing on ethnographic fieldwork conducted in Tokyo and Shanghai from the 1990s to the present, this talk explores how bars contribute to the "cumulative texture" of urban neighborhoods and how these everyday spaces evolve alongside rapidly changing cities.


The discussion will compare the different challenges faced by neighborhood bars in Tokyo and Shanghai.


In Shanghai, once-thriving organic bar streets, including areas such as Maoming Road and Yongkang Road, have faced pressures from urban redevelopment, noise regulation, and state-led gentrification.


In Tokyo, surviving neighborhood spaces such as yokochō continue to maintain their communal character through affordable rents, grassroots activism, and adaptive entrepreneurship, while also facing challenges from rising rents, aging ownership, and urban renewal.


Through comparison between the two cities, the talk examines how property systems, governance approaches, and cultural attitudes toward urban heritage shape the future of neighborhood third places.
