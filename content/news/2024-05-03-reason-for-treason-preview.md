# Reason for Treason


![Event Poster](/images/events/reason-for-treason-2025/poster.png)


The Duke Kunshan University Colloquium Committee (UCC) presents:

**"Reason for Treason"**

featuring Professor Zhihao Xu from Tsinghua University.


This talk examines why military officers change their loyalties during wartime, exploring the internal and external motivations behind defections through the lens of economic history and political economy.


Using newly collected historical data, Professor Xu investigates how career opportunities, institutional backgrounds, and foreign experiences shaped the decisions of high-ranking military officers during the Second Sino-Japanese War.

# Talk Overview


Defections in wars represent extreme forms of loyalty transformation. This talk explores the question:

**What makes military officers betray their country and serve an invading power?**


Drawing on a novel dataset containing the career paths of more than 2,800 high-ranking Nationalist (KMT) military officers—including colonels and generals—during the Second Sino-Japanese War, Professor Xu examines cases of defection to Japanese-supported regimes.


The study identifies three major findings.


## Career Advancement and the Push Effect


High-ranking KMT officers who experienced slower career advancement, either through individual promotions or relative comparisons with their peers, were more likely to defect.


This suggests that limited career prospects created internal pressures that contributed to loyalty transformation.


## Institutional Background and Expected Career Prospects


Officers who graduated from Chiang Kai-shek's mainstream Whampoa Military Academy were less likely to defect, partly because they anticipated stronger career opportunities within existing institutions.


## Foreign Education and the Pull Effect


Officers who received military education in Japan were more likely to defect, as they tended to have more positive impressions of Japan and stronger expectations of favorable treatment under Japanese-supported regimes.


---

# Research Significance


By examining both internal pressures and external incentives, this research highlights the coexistence of push and pull factors behind loyalty transformation.


The talk provides insights into how individual incentives, institutional structures, and historical contexts interact to shape decisions during periods of political and military upheaval.
