# Event Recap:
# Gender, Robots, and Global Commons


![Forum Banner](/images/events/computational-social-science-2025/poster.png)


On March 8, 2025, Duke Kunshan University hosted the **Interdisciplinary Forum on Computational Social Science: "Gender, Robots, and Global Commons — Cross-Disciplinary Perspectives on Ethics, Equity, and Socio-Technical Futures."**


Co-hosted by the Duke Kunshan University Colloquium Committee (UCC), the Center for the Study of Contemporary China, the Division of Social Sciences, and the SocioTech Future Lab, the forum brought together scholars, researchers, and students from multiple disciplines to discuss how computational technologies can contribute to more inclusive, responsible, and sustainable futures.


Rather than focusing only on technological advancement, the forum examined the social responsibilities, ethical questions, and governance challenges surrounding intelligent systems.

# Opening Remarks:
# Bridging Experiences and Institutions


The forum opened with reflections on digital transformation, interdisciplinary collaboration, and the importance of connecting technological innovation with human experiences.


Speakers highlighted how computational social science can help society understand and govern rapidly evolving technologies, especially as artificial intelligence becomes increasingly integrated into everyday life.


![Opening Session](/images/events/computational-social-science-2025/group.png)


---

# Morning Keynote:
# Energy, Information, and the Evolution of Intelligence


Prof. Soo Hong Chew delivered the morning keynote, exploring intelligence as a process shaped by optimization, energy, and value constraints.


His talk examined the relationship between biological intelligence, computational systems, and the challenges of governing increasingly powerful technologies.


![Morning Keynote](/images/events/computational-social-science-2025/keynote.png)

# Panel Discussions


## Panel 1:
## Computational Science for Human Factors in Future Society


This panel explored how computational science interacts with human experience through topics including:

- Human-AI collaboration
- Health informatics
- Affective computing
- Accessibility-oriented technology


The speakers emphasized moving beyond purely functional technological design toward approaches that consider emotion, accessibility, and human needs.


![Panel 1](/images/events/computational-social-science-2025/panel1.png)


---

## Panel 2:
## Gender and Inclusion at the Interplay of Computational Social Science


The discussion examined how gender bias can become embedded within data, algorithms, and research structures.


Panelists explored whether emerging technologies, including generative AI, can reduce barriers to participation while emphasizing the importance of structural change and inclusive methodologies.


![Panel 2](/images/events/computational-social-science-2025/panel2.png)


---

## Panel 3:
## Interdisciplinary Big Data for Global Commons Governance


Researchers discussed how big data systems influence areas including:

- Urban development
- Public health
- Cultural analysis
- Historical research


The panel highlighted the importance of culturally situated approaches and addressing biases embedded within computational systems.

![Panel 3](/images/events/computational-social-science-2025/panel3.png)

# Student Research Showcase


The forum also featured undergraduate presentations exploring topics across:

- Urban informatics
- Sustainable social entrepreneurship
- Health equity
- Computational aesthetics


Student projects demonstrated how technical methods can be combined with social inquiry to address real-world challenges.


![Student Showcase](/images/events/computational-social-science-2025/students.png)


---

# Student Leadership Reflections


The forum was also shaped by strong undergraduate leadership, with students serving as co-chairs, panel organizers, and presenters.


Zhonghan Dai, Student Co-Chair of the forum, reflected on how the event expanded his understanding of computational social science:


> "Figuring out how to make technology genuinely serve humanity is the critical question we must quietly and rigorously reflect upon."


The event demonstrated the value of interdisciplinary collaboration and the importance of developing technologies with social responsibility at their core.


---

# A Forum Beyond Disciplines


The Interdisciplinary Forum on Computational Social Science created a space where computer science, social science, humanities, and policy perspectives could meet.


By connecting technological innovation with questions of ethics, equity, and governance, the forum highlighted DKU's commitment to building responsible futures in the age of intelligent systems.
