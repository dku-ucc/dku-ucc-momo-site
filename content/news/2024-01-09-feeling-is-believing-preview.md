# Talk Overview


What makes people believe?


How do science and disinformation compete to convince us?


And why has the apocalyptic discourse of conspiracy theory risen to prominence in the current political moment in the United States?


In this talk, Professor Schaefer introduces a new approach to understanding belief formation by examining the relationship between thinking and feeling.


Rather than viewing thought and emotion as fundamentally separate, this approach considers them as deeply interconnected dimensions of human experience.


By shifting this perspective, the talk provides a clearer understanding of how and why conspiracy theories have gained such influence in contemporary society.


---

# Research Background


Professor Schaefer's research examines the role of affect and power in the formation of secularism, religion, science, and material culture.


He is the author of:


*Religious Affects: Animality, Evolution, and Power*  
(Duke University Press, 2015)


and


*Wild Experiment: Feeling Science and Secularism after Darwin*  
(Duke University Press, 2022)


*Wild Experiment* received the Ludwik Fleck Prize from the Society for Social Studies of Science (4S) and the annual book prize from the International Society for Science and Religion.
