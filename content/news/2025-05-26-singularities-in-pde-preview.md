# Singularities in PDE


![Event Poster](/images/events/singularities-in-pde-2025/poster.png)


The Duke Kunshan University Colloquium Committee (UCC), together with the Zu Chongzhi Distinguished Lecture Series, presents:

**"Singularities in PDE"**

featuring Professor Alexander Kiselev from Duke University.


Partial differential equations (PDE) have revolutionized science since the development of calculus and serve as powerful models for a broad range of natural and engineering processes.


In this talk, Professor Kiselev will introduce the fascinating phenomenon of singularities in PDE solutions and explore their significance in understanding extreme phenomena, changes in physical regimes, and limitations of mathematical models.

# Speaker


## Alexander Kiselev


Professor Alexander Kiselev is the William T. Laprade Distinguished Professor at Duke University.


He received his BS degree from Saint-Petersburg State University in 1992 and his PhD from Caltech in 1997.


After a postdoctoral position at the Mathematical Sciences Research Institute (MSRI), he held academic positions at the University of Chicago, University of Wisconsin–Madison, and Rice University before joining Duke University in 2017.

# Talk Overview


Partial differential equations provide mathematical frameworks for describing phenomena ranging from physical systems to engineering processes. However, solutions to PDEs can sometimes develop singularities—points where the behavior of a system becomes extreme or where existing models may break down.


This talk will provide an overview of examples of singularity formation, with particular attention to problems in fluid mechanics.


Fluids surround us in everyday life, modern technology, and dramatic natural events such as tornadoes and hurricanes. Despite extensive progress in fluid mechanics, many fundamental questions remain unresolved.


Richard Feynman famously described turbulence as one of the greatest unsolved problems in classical physics. Meanwhile, one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems established by the Clay Mathematics Institute concerns the existence of singularities in the three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations, the foundational model of fluid mechanics.


Designed for a non-specialist audience, this talk will explore the connections between these major challenges and introduce key themes in the study of fluids and PDE.
