# Talk Overview


This talk provides a critical overview of recent developments in the study of human decision-making patterns in behavioral economics, neuroeconomics, and agent-based modeling.


Dr. Zhao introduces a macro-sociological perspective inspired by an important theory in evolutionary biology — the **r/K strategy theory** — to examine how human strategic choices interact with changing social contexts.


The talk outlines five patterned relationships between dominant strategies and social environments, providing a framework for understanding how individuals and organizations make strategic choices.


Furthermore, it explores how the unique characteristics of Homo sapiens have contributed to increasingly complex interactions between human behavior and environmental conditions.


---

# Research Perspective


Drawing primarily from observations of China's socioeconomic transformations over recent decades, Dr. Zhao examines how changes in social environments correspond with shifts in dominant strategic patterns among Chinese society.


Through these examples, the talk demonstrates the explanatory power and analytical value of the r/K strategy framework compared with other decision-making theories commonly used in the social sciences.
